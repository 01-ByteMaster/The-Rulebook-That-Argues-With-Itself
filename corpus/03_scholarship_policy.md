# Scholarship Policy — Ridgeview University

This policy governs eligibility, disbursement, and renewal of university-administered merit and need-based scholarships. It does not cover externally funded scholarships (government or corporate), which are governed by the terms of their respective sponsoring bodies.

## SCH-1: Eligibility Criteria
<!-- section_id: SCH-1 | conflict_group: null -->

University scholarships are available to full-time undergraduate and postgraduate students who meet at least one of the following criteria at the time of application: admission in the top 10% of their entrance examination cohort (merit category), documented family income below the threshold set annually by the Financial Aid Office (need category), or recognized achievement in sports, arts, or other extracurricular domains at the state or national level (talent category). Students may apply under only one category per academic year, though the Financial Aid Office may, at its discretion, consider a merit applicant for supplementary need-based aid where financial hardship is separately documented. First-year students are assessed on entrance examination performance and documentation submitted at admission; continuing students are assessed on prior academic performance at Ridgeview as described in SCH-4-GPA-A and SCH-6-GPA-B.

## SCH-2: Application Process
<!-- section_id: SCH-2 | conflict_group: null -->

Scholarship applications are submitted through the student portal during the designated application window, which opens four weeks before the start of each academic year for new applicants and two weeks before the start of each semester for renewal applicants. Required documentation varies by category but generally includes: prior academic transcripts, income certificates (need category), or achievement certificates issued by a recognized sporting or cultural body (talent category). Incomplete applications are held in a pending queue for up to ten working days, during which the applicant may be contacted to supply missing documents; applications still incomplete after this period are closed and must be resubmitted in the next application window. The Financial Aid Office aims to communicate decisions within four weeks of the application window closing, though volume in peak periods (start of academic year) may extend this to six weeks.

## SCH-3: Disbursement Schedule
<!-- section_id: SCH-3 | conflict_group: null -->

Approved scholarships are disbursed in two installments per academic year, aligned with the two-semester structure: the first installment is credited within three weeks of the start of the odd semester, and the second within three weeks of the start of the even semester, provided the student's renewal status (see SCH-4-GPA-A) has been confirmed. Disbursement is made directly against the student's tuition and hostel fee account where the scholarship covers those costs, with any remaining stipend amount (for scholarships that include a living allowance) credited to the student's registered bank account. Students who withdraw from the university mid-semester after receiving a disbursement are required to refund the pro-rated unused portion, calculated from the date of formal withdrawal.

## SCH-4-GPA-A: Renewal Requirement — Academic Standing
<!-- section_id: SCH-4-GPA-A | conflict_group: CG-SCHOLARSHIP-RENEWAL -->

To renew a scholarship for the following academic year, students must maintain a Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) of at least 6.5 out of 10 in each academic year. This requirement applies uniformly across merit, need, and talent categories once a student has been awarded a scholarship — the academic standing bar does not vary by the category under which the scholarship was originally granted. CGPA is assessed at the end of the second semester of each academic year, using the university's standard grade point conversion table. A student whose CGPA falls below 6.5 at that checkpoint is placed on a one-semester probation period (see SCH-5) rather than having the scholarship immediately terminated, giving them an opportunity to recover their standing before renewal is formally denied.

## SCH-5: Grounds for Suspension or Cancellation
<!-- section_id: SCH-5 | conflict_group: null -->

A scholarship may be suspended or cancelled on any of the following grounds: failure to meet the renewal academic standing requirement after the probation period described in SCH-4-GPA-A, a finding of academic dishonesty by the university's Academic Integrity Committee, withdrawal or dismissal from the university, or discovery that the original application contained materially false information (income misrepresentation, fabricated achievement certificates, and similar). Suspension differs from cancellation in that a suspended scholarship may be reinstated if the underlying condition is resolved within the probation window, while cancellation is final for the remainder of the student's enrollment and requires a fresh application in a subsequent year, subject to eligibility. Students facing suspension or cancellation are notified in writing and given the opportunity to submit a written response before the decision is finalized by the Financial Aid Office.

## SCH-6-GPA-B: Continuing Eligibility — Satisfactory Academic Standing
<!-- section_id: SCH-6-GPA-B | conflict_group: CG-SCHOLARSHIP-RENEWAL -->

To be considered in satisfactory academic standing for continued scholarship support, a student must achieve a CGPA of 7.0 or above at the end of each semester. This standard reflects the university's expectation that scholarship recipients demonstrate consistent academic performance every semester, not merely at the annual renewal checkpoint, and is assessed independently by the Office of the Registrar as part of the semester-end academic review that all students undergo regardless of scholarship status. Students whose CGPA falls below 7.0 in any given semester are flagged in the Registrar's satisfactory-standing report, which is shared with the Financial Aid Office for consideration alongside the renewal process. Continued scholarship support is contingent on remaining above this threshold, and the Registrar's semester-end review is the university's formal record of a student's standing for all academic and financial-aid purposes.

## SCH-7: Appeals and Reinstatement
<!-- section_id: SCH-7 | conflict_group: null -->

A student whose scholarship is suspended or cancelled may file an appeal with the Scholarship Appeals Committee within fifteen working days of receiving written notice. Appeals should be accompanied by supporting documentation relevant to the grounds for suspension or cancellation — for example, medical documentation explaining a drop in academic performance, or evidence disputing an academic integrity finding. The Committee, comprising two faculty representatives and one member of the Financial Aid Office, reviews the appeal and communicates its decision within twenty working days. Where an appeal is upheld, the scholarship is reinstated retroactively for the semester in question, including any withheld disbursement; where an appeal is denied, the student may make one further submission to the Dean of Student Affairs, whose decision is final.

## SCH-8: Interaction with Other Financial Aid
<!-- section_id: SCH-8 | conflict_group: null -->

A student receiving a university scholarship under this policy may simultaneously hold an external scholarship or education loan, provided the combined value of all financial aid does not exceed the total cost of tuition, hostel, and mess fees for the academic year; where the combined value would exceed this cap, the university scholarship amount is reduced first, since external awards are generally governed by their own non-reduction terms. Students are required to declare any external scholarship or aid they receive to the Financial Aid Office within two weeks of the award being confirmed, regardless of whether it affects the cap described above, so that the university's records remain accurate for institutional reporting purposes. Failure to declare an external award that results in the student exceeding the aid cap is treated as a material omission and may be grounds for scholarship suspension under SCH-5, even where the omission was unintentional, though the Financial Aid Office will generally issue a correction notice and an opportunity to adjust before escalating to a formal suspension review.
