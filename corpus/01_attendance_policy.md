# Attendance Policy — Ridgeview University

This document governs attendance requirements for all undergraduate and postgraduate students enrolled in on-campus programs at Ridgeview University. It is binding for all departments unless a department-specific policy has been separately approved by the Academic Council.

## ATT-1: Purpose and Scope
<!-- section_id: ATT-1 | conflict_group: null -->

The purpose of this policy is to ensure that students engage consistently with classroom instruction, laboratory work, and tutorials, and to establish a uniform standard by which exam eligibility is determined across all schools of the university. This policy applies to every credit-bearing course a student is registered for in a given semester, including electives, audit-converted courses, and courses taken through inter-departmental exchange. It does not apply to non-credit workshops, orientation sessions, or university-wide events, attendance at which is tracked separately and does not factor into examination eligibility. Attendance requirements are enforced independently for each course; a student may meet the requirement in one course and fall short in another within the same semester, and eligibility is assessed course-by-course rather than as a semester-wide average.

## ATT-2: Minimum Attendance Requirement
<!-- section_id: ATT-2 | conflict_group: CG-ATTENDANCE -->

Students must maintain 75% attendance to be eligible to sit for the semester examination. This threshold is calculated per course and applies uniformly across all programs and years of study. A student whose attendance in any single course falls below 75% at the close of the semester is, by default, ineligible to appear for that course's end-semester examination, regardless of academic performance in internal assessments. The 75% figure has been the university's standard eligibility floor since the policy was last revised by the Academic Council, and it is the benchmark referenced on admit cards, in the student handbook summary, and in departmental orientation material. Attendance is monitored on a rolling basis throughout the semester so that students can track their standing well before the examination period begins, rather than being informed only after the cutoff has already been missed.

## ATT-3: How Attendance Is Calculated
<!-- section_id: ATT-3 | conflict_group: null -->

Attendance percentage for a course is calculated as the number of classes attended divided by the total number of classes conducted for that course up to the point of calculation, expressed as a percentage and rounded to the nearest whole number. Classes conducted include lectures, tutorials, and scheduled laboratory sessions, each counted as a single attendance unit regardless of duration. Attendance is recorded through the biometric check-in system installed in each classroom and laboratory; students are responsible for checking in within the first ten minutes of a session, after which the system locks and manual entry requires instructor sign-off. Classes cancelled by the instructor or the university (including cancellations due to public holidays announced after the semester calendar was published) are excluded from both the numerator and denominator and do not count against a student's percentage. Attendance percentages are visible to students in real time through the student portal, updated within 24 hours of each recorded session.

## ATT-4: Attendance Shortage Notification
<!-- section_id: ATT-4 | conflict_group: null -->

The university operates a tiered notification system to alert students before attendance shortages become irreversible. A first-level advisory is issued automatically through the student portal when a student's attendance in any course drops below 85%. A second-level warning, copied to the student's academic advisor and department office, is issued when attendance drops below 80%. Once attendance falls below 75%, a formal shortage notice is generated and the student is required to meet with their academic advisor within five working days to discuss options, which may include a condonation request (see ATT-7) or a plan for the remainder of the semester. Notices are sent to the student's official university email address and are also visible on the portal dashboard; failure to check email or the portal does not constitute grounds for later disputing that notice was given.

## ATT-5-MEDICAL: Medical Exemption
<!-- section_id: ATT-5-MEDICAL | conflict_group: CG-ATTENDANCE -->

Students with an approved medical certificate require only 60% attendance for exam eligibility. This reduced threshold recognizes that students recovering from illness, undergoing treatment, or managing a documented health condition may be unable to meet the standard 75% requirement through no fault of their own. To qualify, the medical certificate must be issued by a registered medical practitioner, must specify the period of incapacity, and must be submitted to the university's Medical Exemption Cell (see ATT-6) before the end of the semester in which the shortfall occurs. Once approved, the 60% threshold applies specifically to the courses affected by the documented period of illness; it is not a blanket reduction applied to a student's entire course load unless the medical documentation supports incapacity across the full semester. Students should note that the medical exemption reduces the eligibility floor — it does not exempt a student from attendance recordkeeping, and attendance below even the reduced 60% threshold still results in ineligibility.

## ATT-6: Medical Certificate Submission Procedure
<!-- section_id: ATT-6 | conflict_group: null -->

Medical certificates supporting an exemption request under ATT-5-MEDICAL must be submitted in original or certified-copy form to the Medical Exemption Cell, located in the Student Affairs building, within fifteen working days of the student's return to campus following the period of illness. Certificates submitted after this window may still be considered at the discretion of the Dean of Student Affairs but are not guaranteed acceptance. The submission must include: the practitioner's registration number, the specific dates of incapacity, a diagnosis or general description of the condition sufficient to justify the duration claimed, and the practitioner's official stamp and signature. The Medical Exemption Cell reviews submissions on a rolling basis and communicates its decision to the student and the relevant department office within ten working days of receipt. Students undergoing extended treatment (exceeding four weeks) should submit interim certificates covering each phase of treatment rather than waiting until full recovery to submit a single consolidated document.

## ATT-7: Attendance Appeals Process
<!-- section_id: ATT-7 | conflict_group: null -->

A student who believes their recorded attendance is inaccurate, or who wishes to request condonation of a shortfall not covered by a medical exemption, may file an appeal with the Attendance Appeals Committee. Appeals must be submitted in writing through the student portal within ten working days of the shortage notice described in ATT-4. The Committee, comprising two faculty members and one representative from Student Affairs, reviews supporting evidence such as biometric logs, instructor correspondence, or documentation of extenuating circumstances (bereavement, family emergency, university-sponsored travel for sports or academic representation, and similar situations not covered by the medical exemption). The Committee's decision is communicated within fifteen working days and is final at the departmental level; students dissatisfied with the outcome may escalate to the Dean of Academic Affairs, whose decision is final. Condonation granted through this process is recorded against the specific course and semester and does not set a precedent for future semesters.

## ATT-8: Consequences of Attendance Shortage
<!-- section_id: ATT-8 | conflict_group: null -->

A student who does not meet the applicable attendance threshold and has not been granted a medical exemption or committee condonation is barred from sitting the end-semester examination for the affected course. This is recorded on the academic transcript as "Detained — Attendance Shortage" rather than as a failing grade, and does not count against the student's grade point average, though it does delay progression and may affect scholarship or hostel continuation eligibility as governed by their respective policies. A detained student must re-register for and repeat the course in a subsequent semester, subject to the standard re-registration fee. Students detained in three or more courses across their academic career are referred to the Dean of Academic Affairs for a broader review of their continuation in the program.

## ATT-9: Attendance for Practical, Lab, and Field-Based Courses
<!-- section_id: ATT-9 | conflict_group: null -->

Courses with a laboratory, studio, or field-based component are subject to the same threshold structure described in ATT-2 and ATT-5-MEDICAL, but attendance for the practical component is tracked separately from the associated theory lecture where the course is split into distinct theory and practical heads on the academic calendar. A student may meet the attendance requirement for a course's theory component while falling short on the practical component, or vice versa, and in such cases eligibility to sit the end-semester examination is assessed independently for each component; a shortfall in the practical component alone can still result in detention for that course even where theory attendance is fully compliant. Field trips and off-campus practical sessions that are a mandatory part of the syllabus count toward attendance in the same manner as an on-campus session, provided the student's presence is confirmed by the accompanying faculty member's sign-off sheet rather than the classroom biometric system, since biometric infrastructure is not available at off-campus sites. Students unable to attend a mandatory field session for documented medical reasons should follow the same certificate submission procedure described in ATT-6, and the session is then treated as excluded from the denominator in the same way a cancelled class would be, rather than counted as an absence.
