# Hostel Handbook — Ridgeview University

This handbook governs residence in all university-operated hostels and applies to every student allotted a room, regardless of program or year of study. Students are required to acknowledge having read this handbook at the time of hostel check-in.

## HOSTEL-1: Admission and Room Allotment
<!-- section_id: HOSTEL-1 | conflict_group: null -->

Hostel accommodation is allotted on a first-come, first-served basis among eligible applicants, subject to reserved quotas for first-year students, students with documented medical needs, and outstation students residing more than 150 kilometers from campus. Applications for hostel accommodation open at the start of each academic year and must be accompanied by the hostel admission fee and a signed acknowledgment of this handbook. Room allotments are made by the Hostel Office and are generally fixed for the academic year, though students may apply for a room transfer through the Hostel Office under specific circumstances such as documented conflict with a roommate or a change in medical accommodation needs. Allotment does not guarantee the same room or block in a subsequent year; students must reapply each academic year.

## HOSTEL-2: Curfew and Entry/Exit Timings
<!-- section_id: HOSTEL-2 | conflict_group: null -->

All hostel residents must be inside the hostel premises by 10:30 PM on weekdays and 11:30 PM on weekends and university holidays. Entry after these times requires sign-in at the security desk with a valid reason recorded in the late-entry register; three or more unexplained late entries in a semester result in a formal warning from the Hostel Warden. Students requiring late entry for university-sanctioned reasons — laboratory work, sports practice, or academic events — must carry a signed permission slip from the relevant faculty member or coach. Overnight absence from the hostel requires prior written permission from the Warden, and undergraduate students under 18 additionally require a parent or guardian's acknowledgment on file. Repeated unauthorized overnight absence is treated as a disciplinary matter under HOSTEL-8.

## HOSTEL-3: Guest and Visitor Policy
<!-- section_id: HOSTEL-3 | conflict_group: null -->

Visitors are permitted in the designated common areas of the hostel between 10:00 AM and 7:00 PM daily, and must sign in at the security desk with a valid photo ID, which is retained at the desk for the duration of the visit. Visitors are not permitted in student rooms under any circumstances; all visits must take place in the common room, visitor's lounge, or hostel lawn. Overnight guests are not permitted except for parents or legal guardians, who may stay in the designated guest rooms (subject to availability and a nominal guest-room fee) with prior notice to the Hostel Office of at least 24 hours. Students found hosting unauthorized overnight guests, or hosting any visitor in their room, are subject to a fine and, on repeat occurrence, disciplinary review under HOSTEL-8.

## HOSTEL-4-REFUND-A: Security Deposit Refund on Early Checkout
<!-- section_id: HOSTEL-4-REFUND-A | conflict_group: CG-HOSTEL-REFUND -->

A student who vacates hostel accommodation before the end of the academic year — whether due to withdrawal from the university, transfer to off-campus housing, or program completion ahead of schedule — is entitled to a refund of their security deposit, less any deductions for outstanding dues or documented room damage. Refunds are processed within 15 working days of the student vacating the room and submitting a completed no-dues certificate, countersigned by the Hostel Warden, the mess office, and the library. The 15-working-day window begins on the date all three sign-offs are obtained, not on the date the student physically moves out, so students are encouraged to complete the no-dues process promptly to avoid delay. Refunds are credited to the bank account on file with the Accounts Office; students who have not registered a bank account with the university must do so before a refund can be disbursed.

## HOSTEL-5: Damages, Fines, and Recoveries
<!-- section_id: HOSTEL-5 | conflict_group: null -->

Students are financially responsible for damage to hostel property beyond normal wear and tear, including damage to furniture, fixtures, and shared common-area equipment. Damage is assessed by the Hostel Maintenance team at the time it is reported or discovered, and the assessed cost is charged to the responsible student's account; where responsibility cannot be attributed to a specific individual, the cost may be divided among the residents of the affected room or floor. A standard fine schedule applies to common infractions: unauthorized appliances (₹500 per instance), smoking or vaping within hostel premises (₹1,000 per instance, plus disciplinary referral), and unauthorized room modifications such as drilling into walls (cost of repair plus a ₹500 administrative fee). Students may appeal a damage assessment to the Hostel Office within seven working days of being charged, providing evidence such as photographs from move-in inspection where available.

## HOSTEL-6: Mess Fees and Meal Plans
<!-- section_id: HOSTEL-6 | conflict_group: null -->

Hostel residents are enrolled by default in the standard mess meal plan, which covers breakfast, lunch, and dinner seven days a week and is billed as a fixed semester fee alongside hostel fees. Students with documented dietary restrictions may request the modified meal plan (vegetarian, Jain, or medically restricted) at no additional cost by submitting a request to the Mess Committee within the first two weeks of the semester. Students who wish to opt out of the mess plan entirely — for example, to self-cater or use off-campus meal options — must apply in writing to the Hostel Office before the semester begins; opt-outs are not accepted mid-semester except on medical grounds certified by the university health center. Mess fees, once paid for the semester, are not refunded for individual missed meals, though extended absence exceeding two continuous weeks (for medical leave, university-sponsored travel, or similar) may be credited toward the following semester's mess fee upon application.

## HOSTEL-7: Room Maintenance and Complaints
<!-- section_id: HOSTEL-7 | conflict_group: null -->

Maintenance requests — plumbing, electrical, furniture repair, pest control, and similar — should be logged through the Hostel Office's online complaint portal, which assigns each request a tracking number and a target resolution window (48 hours for urgent issues such as electrical faults or water leakage, five working days for routine repairs). Students may also report issues directly to their floor's Resident Assistant, who escalates to Maintenance on the student's behalf. Rooms are subject to a routine inspection once per semester, conducted with 48 hours' advance notice, to check for safety compliance, unauthorized appliances, and general upkeep; unannounced inspections may occur only where the Warden has reasonable cause to believe a safety violation is in progress. Persistent unresolved maintenance issues beyond the target resolution window should be escalated in writing to the Hostel Office for priority handling.

## HOSTEL-8: Disciplinary Actions and Room Cancellation
<!-- section_id: HOSTEL-8 | conflict_group: null -->

Violations of this handbook are addressed through a graduated disciplinary process: a first violation typically results in a written warning recorded in the student's hostel file; a second violation of the same or a related nature results in a fine and mandatory meeting with the Warden; a third violation, or any single serious violation (such as physical altercation, possession of prohibited substances, or repeated unauthorized guests), may result in suspension of hostel privileges or cancellation of the room allotment for the remainder of the academic year. A student whose hostel allotment is cancelled for disciplinary reasons is required to vacate within seven days and is subject to the standard refund process under HOSTEL-4-REFUND-A for any recoverable deposit, less applicable fines and dues. Disciplinary decisions may be appealed in writing to the Dean of Student Affairs within ten working days of the decision being communicated.

## HOSTEL-9: End-of-Year Vacation and Room Retention
<!-- section_id: HOSTEL-9 | conflict_group: null -->

Hostel rooms must be vacated within three days of a student's final examination of the academic year, except for students who have been granted vacation-period retention for a documented reason such as an internship requiring continued campus residence, participation in a university-sponsored competition, or ongoing thesis or project work requiring lab access. Requests for vacation-period retention must be submitted to the Hostel Office at least two weeks before the examination period ends and are approved on a case-by-case basis subject to room availability, since vacation-period maintenance and deep-cleaning is scheduled block-by-block once a block is fully vacated. Students granted retention are charged a prorated daily rate for the retention period, billed separately from the standard semester hostel fee, and remain subject to all provisions of this handbook, including curfew (HOSTEL-2) and guest policy (HOSTEL-3), for the duration of their stay. Personal belongings left in a room beyond the vacate deadline without approved retention status are moved to university storage at the student's risk and expense, and the university is not liable for loss or damage to items left unattended in an unlocked or improperly secured room after the vacate deadline has passed.
